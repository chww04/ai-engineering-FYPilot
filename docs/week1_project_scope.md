# FYPilot — AI FYP Topic Recommendation Assistant

| Field | Details |
| --- | --- |
| **Project Name** | FYPilot — AI FYP Topic Recommendation Assistant |
| **Domain** | Higher Education / Final Year Project (FYP) Planning |
| **Problem Statement** | Final-year students struggle to choose a suitable, feasible FYP topic and matching supervisor. Topic lists, lecturer research areas, and past FYP titles are scattered across separate PDFs and web pages, and supervisors have limited time for one-to-one topic consultation.<br><br>AI engineering makes it possible to build a document-grounded assistant that recommends and explains FYP topics based on a student's interests and skills, citing official faculty documents. |
| **Evidence of the Problem** | 1. A survey of 150 undergraduates found about 40% were unhappy or mixed about how they chose their final-year project; some felt unprepared to choose or had too little information to choose well ([Winder, 2023](https://distantreader.org/stacks/journals/jldhe/jldhe-923.pdf)).<br>2. Researchers at UiTM Perlis, Malaysia, note that final-year students struggle to find suitable supervisors because they often don't know lecturers' areas of expertise, which can lead to project titles that don't match their interests ([Ismail et al., arXiv:1908.03475](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.03475)).<br>3. A 2026 study states that choosing an appropriate thesis topic is challenging when it doesn't match a student's skills, interests, and experience, and built a KNN-based topic recommender to address it ([Maazzalla & Perkasa, IJSECS 2026](https://journal.lembagakita.com/ijsecs/article/download/7659/5032)). |
| **Initial Related Work / Existing Approaches** | 1. Supervisor consultation — personalised, but constrained by lecturer availability and the student's awareness of who supervises what.<br>2. Static faculty topic lists / FYP handbooks — authoritative, but fragmented, not searchable by interest, and quickly outdated.<br>3. General-purpose AI chatbots (e.g. ChatGPT) — creative, but not grounded in the faculty's actual supervisors, rules, or past projects; may suggest infeasible or duplicate topics.<br>4. FYP management systems — handle submissions and approvals, but do not recommend topics. |
| **Engineering Gap / Limitation** | No existing approach combines (a) personalised recommendation from a student's interests and skills with (b) grounding in official faculty documents — FYP rules, supervisor expertise, and past projects — with (c) transparent citations and novelty checks against previous FYPs. |
| **Initial Project Objectives** | **O1:** To design a document-grounded AI assistant that recommends FYP topics and matching supervisors using official faculty and repository documents.<br>**O2:** To develop the solution using retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) with hybrid search, source citations, a past-project similarity check, and guardrails.<br>**O3:** To evaluate the solution in terms of recommendation relevance, groundedness (citation accuracy), novelty vs. past FYPs, latency, and safety. |
| **Target Users** | Final-year (and pre-final-year) undergraduate students in computing / AI programmes preparing their FYP proposal.<br>Secondary: FYP coordinators and supervisors who want to point students to a self-service first step. |
| **Data Sources** | **Source 1:** Faculty FYP Guidelines / Handbook PDF — scope rules, timelines, assessment criteria.<br>**Source 2:** UTM Faculty of AI research group pages ([fai.utm.my](https://fai.utm.my)) and lecturer profile pages ([people.utm.my](https://people.utm.my)) — supervisor expertise areas.<br>**Source 3:** Past project and thesis abstracts from the UTM Institutional Repository ([eprints.utm.my](https://eprints.utm.my)) — topic inspiration and duplicate/novelty checks.<br>**Optional Source 4:** Recent arXiv abstracts in AI/ML for emerging-topic suggestions. |
| **Similar to EduAdvisor** | Partially. Like EduAdvisor, it is a document-grounded RAG assistant over university PDFs. It differs by being a recommender: it takes a student profile (interests, skills, preferred tools) as input, ranks topics and supervisors, and checks novelty against past projects, rather than only answering policy questions. |
| **Responsible AI Risks** | **Risk 1 — Hallucination:** Model invents a supervisor's expertise or a non-existent past project → student approaches the wrong supervisor or proposes an infeasible topic. *Control:* answer only from retrieved chunks, mandatory citations, "not found" fallback.<br>**Risk 2 — Fairness / popularity bias:** Supervisors or areas with richer online profiles get recommended more → uneven supervisor load and narrower topic diversity. *Control:* diversity re-ranking, coverage audit across supervisors.<br>**Risk 3 — Over-reliance / academic integrity:** Students copy generated topics or proposal text as their own → weak ownership and possible plagiarism. *Control:* system suggests directions and questions, never full proposals; disclaimer to confirm with supervisor.<br>**Risk 4 — Privacy:** Students enter personal details (CGPA, name, matric number) → data exposure. *Control:* no account required, do not log profile inputs, strip PII. |
