# FYPilot — AI FYP Topic Recommendation Assistant

| Field | Details |
| --- | --- |
| **Project Name** | FYPilot — AI FYP Topic Recommendation Assistant |
| **Domain** | Higher Education / Final Year Project (FYP) Planning |
| **Problem Statement** | Final-year students struggle to choose a suitable, feasible FYP topic and a matching supervisor, because topic lists, lecturer research areas and past project titles are scattered across separate PDFs and web pages, and supervisors have limited time for one-to-one consultation. General-purpose chatbots can suggest ideas, but they are not grounded in the faculty's real supervisors, rules or past projects. AI engineering makes it possible to build a retrieval-augmented (RAG) assistant that recommends and explains topics using official documents, with citations, guardrails and a novelty check against previous FYPs. |
| **Evidence of the Problem** | 1. A survey of 150 undergraduates found about 40% were unhappy or mixed about how they chose their final-year project; some felt unprepared to choose or had too little information to choose well ([Winder, 2023](https://distantreader.org/stacks/journals/jldhe/jldhe-923.pdf)).<br>2. Researchers at UiTM Perlis, Malaysia, note that final-year students struggle to find suitable supervisors because they often don't know lecturers' areas of expertise, which can lead to project titles that don't match their interests ([Ismail et al., arXiv:1908.03475](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.03475)).<br>3. A 2026 study states that choosing an appropriate thesis topic is challenging when it doesn't match a student's skills, interests, and experience, and built a KNN-based topic recommender to address it ([Maazzalla & Perkasa, IJSECS 2026](https://journal.lembagakita.com/ijsecs/article/download/7659/5032)). |
| **Initial Related Work / Existing Approaches** | 1. **Supervisor recommender engine** — rates a student's interest in fixed areas (e.g. Multimedia, Network, AI) and returns lecturers with similar profiles ([Link](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.03475)). *Limitation:* matches supervisors only by a few fixed categories; it does not suggest specific topics, read faculty documents, or explain its recommendations.<br>2. **KNN thesis topic recommender** — classifies students into topic areas from their skills, interests and experience ([Link](https://journal.lembagakita.com/ijsecs/article/download/7659/5032)). *Limitation:* only four broad areas, a small dataset (25 respondents, 5 test cases), and no grounding in supervisors, past projects or citations.<br>3. **TeamUp: semantic project matching** — embeds student profiles and project descriptions with pretrained language models and ranks projects by cosine similarity, adjusted for difficulty and supervisor demand ([Link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03237)). *Limitation:* matches students to a fixed list of existing projects rather than helping them form a new topic; evaluated only on synthetic data (250 dummy students), with no citations to source documents or novelty check against past projects. |
| **Engineering Gap / Limitation** | Existing recommenders match students only by a few fixed categories or to a fixed list of existing projects, and general-purpose chatbots are not grounded in faculty data. None combines (a) personalised topic recommendation from a student's interests and skills with (b) grounding in official faculty documents — FYP rules, supervisor expertise and past projects — and (c) transparent citations and a novelty check against previous FYPs. |
| **Initial Project Objectives** | **O1:** To design a document-grounded AI assistant that recommends FYP topics and matching supervisors using official faculty and repository documents.<br>**O2:** To develop the solution using retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) with hybrid search, source citations, a past-project similarity check, and guardrails.<br>**O3:** To evaluate the solution in terms of recommendation relevance, groundedness (citation accuracy), novelty against past FYPs, latency, and safety. |
| **Target Users** | Undergraduate students at UTM's Faculty of Artificial Intelligence who are about to choose their FYP topic and supervisor, especially those who are unsure which research area suits their interests and skills or which lecturer supervises it. |
| **Data Sources** | **Source 1:** FAI FYP Guidelines (UTM Faculty of AI FYP site) — scope rules, timeline, assessment criteria — [sites.google.com/utm.my/fai-fyp/home](https://sites.google.com/utm.my/fai-fyp/home)<br>**Source 2:** UTM Institutional Repository — past project and thesis abstracts for topic inspiration and novelty checks — [eprints.utm.my](https://eprints.utm.my)<br>**Source 3:** UTM Faculty of Artificial Intelligence — departments and research groups — [fai.utm.my](https://fai.utm.my)<br>**Source 4:** UTM staff profile pages — lecturer research expertise for supervisor matching — [people.utm.my](https://people.utm.my)<br>**Optional Source 5:** arXiv (cs.AI / cs.CL) — recent abstracts for emerging-topic suggestions — [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org) |
| **Similar to EduAdvisor** | Partially. Like EduAdvisor, it is a document-grounded RAG assistant over university PDFs. It differs by being a recommender: it takes a student profile (interests, skills, preferred tools) as input, ranks topics and supervisors, and checks novelty against past projects, rather than only answering policy questions. |
| **Responsible AI Risks** | **Risk 1 — Hallucination:** Model invents a supervisor's expertise or a non-existent past project → student approaches the wrong supervisor or proposes an infeasible topic. *Control:* answer only from retrieved chunks, mandatory citations, "not found" fallback.<br>**Risk 2 — Fairness / popularity bias:** Supervisors or areas with richer online profiles get recommended more → uneven supervisor load and narrower topic diversity. *Control:* diversity re-ranking, coverage audit across supervisors.<br>**Risk 3 — Over-reliance / academic integrity:** Students copy generated topics or proposal text as their own → weak ownership and possible plagiarism. *Control:* system suggests directions and questions, never full proposals; disclaimer to confirm with supervisor.<br>**Risk 4 — Privacy:** Students enter personal details (CGPA, name, matric number) → data exposure. *Control:* no account required, do not log profile inputs, strip PII. |
